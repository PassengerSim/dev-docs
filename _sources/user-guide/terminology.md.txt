# Terminology

While PassengerSim is designed to be flexible and support a variety of modeling approaches, it is
helpful to have a common set of terms to describe the key concepts. The following definitions are
used throughout the documentation and code. They may not align exactly with the definitions used in
other modeling platforms or for particular airlines or supporting companies, but they are intended
to provide a consistent framework for understanding the PassengerSim model and its outputs.

## Service and Network Structure

The following terminology is used in PassengerSim when describing the air travel services that are
supplied by carriers to customers, and the structure of the airline network:

- **carrier** represents a common carrier that operates vehicles and sells seats to customers. In
  most applications, a carrier is an airline and the vehicles are airplanes, but it could also be a
  bus or train operator, or a combination/hybrid. We often will adopt the language of airlines and
  flights, but the concepts are intended to be more general.

- **place** represents a location where passengers can board or deplane an aircraft (or other
  vehicle). A place may be an airport, a rail station, or some other similar type location. In
  PassengerSim, we generally assume that each place is a distinct origin or destination for
  customers, and that customers don't consider multiple places as the same origin or destination.
  For example, if both MDW and ORD are included in the model, we assume that customers consider each
  airport as a distinct origin or destination, and that they don't consider "Chicago" as a whole as
  their origin or destination.

- **leg** represents a single scheduled flight operated by a carrier, with a specific origin,
  destination, departure time, and arrival time. We generally assume that a leg is operated by a
  single aircraft in a single flight operation, although the key feature is that the beginning and
  end of a leg are defined by the place and time that passengers can board and deplane the aircraft,
  rather than by the physical aircraft operation. For example, a "leg" might include a refueling
  stop, but this is not modeled explicitly in PassengerSim.

- **service** represents the set of legs that are operated by a carrier between two specific places.
  A service may include one or more legs, but these are legs servicing the same places, not
  connecting. A service is defined by the carrier, the origin and destination places. For example, a
  carrier's service from ORD to LAX may include three different flight legs (at different times of
  day).

- **path** represents a sequence of one or more legs that a customer can take to travel from an
  origin to a destination. A path is defined by the sequence of legs, the departure time of the
  first leg, and the arrival time of the last leg. A path may include connections, or it could be a
  single leg without connections. Currently, PassengerSim does not support modeling code-shares, so
  all paths are assumed to be comprised of legs operated by a single carrier.

- **market** represents a pair of origin and destination places that are connected by one or more
  paths. A market is defined by the origin and destination places, and all paths that connect those
  two places are considered to be in the same market. Markets are directional, so the market from
  place A to place B is different from the market from place B to place A.

- **cabin** represents the cabin types on aircraft. Cabins are strictly ordered, so it is possible
  to decide if one cabin is better or worse than another. This ordering is also universal; we assume
  that every customer would order the cabins in the same way, and that every customer would always
  prefer a better cabin to a worse cabin, all else equal.

## Fares and Pricing

PassengerSim offers two paradigms for modeling fares: linear pricing and matrix pricing. In linear
pricing, there is a single one-dimensional ladder of fares for any given market, and the RM system
can open or close each fare on the ladder. In matrix pricing, there is a two-dimensional matrix of
fares. Each cell in this two dimensional matrix may be populated with a fare or not. The RM system
can open or close each *row* in the matrix, but cannot independently open or close each *cell*.
Within this framework, linear pricing can be thought of as a special case of matrix pricing, where
there is only one column in the matrix.

The following terminology is used in PassengerSim code when referring to various aspects of fares
and pricing:

- **booking_class** represents a "row" of the matrix under matrix pricing. A booking class may have
  advance purchase or other restrictions tied to it at the configuration level, but this is for the
  convenience of the user; when we convert from configuration to simulation all advance purchase or
  other restrictions are pushed into the `Fare` objects. Booking classes may also have a minimum
  cabin type attached.

- **brand** represents a "column" of the matrix under matrix pricing. A brand may have restrictions
  tied to it, or ancillaries bundled with it. A brand may have a minimum or maximum booking class
  (e.g. "basic economy" is never offered above booking class "Y2", or "gold elite" is never offered
  below booking class "Y5")

- **fare** is a "cell" in the matrix under matrix pricing. It is the combination of a booking_class
  and a brand. It may also have uniquely defined attributes from a configuration. If a user wants to
  define restrictions that are not homogenous by booking_class or brand, they can do so by
  configuring restrictions at the fare level.

- **fare_class** is a combination of a booking class and a brand. It is used to label the specific
  fare that is being offered to a customer. It is a generalization of the fare: while the `fare` is
  specific to a market and also has other attributes like prices and restrictions, the `fare_class`
  is just a label.

### Prices

In contrast to a "fare", which refers to a combination of price and restrictions, when we are
referring to "price" we are referring to a pure currency amount, without any restrictions attached.
There are various types of "price" values that are relevant to different parts of the code, and it
is important to be precise about which one we are referring to in different contexts.

- **customer_price** is an attribute of an `Offer`. It is the amount of money that a customer pays
  to book that offer, exclusive of any ancillaries that are not bundled into the offer. This is also
  the carrier revenue actually realized if a customer books an offer. This value is specific to each
  offer, and due to continuous pricing effects may not be exactly the same as the `fare_price`. In
  the absence of continuous pricing this value should be the same as the `fare_price`.

- **fare_price** is an attribute of a `Fare`. It is roughly equivalent to a "published" fare for a
  particular combination of booking class and brand, for a given market. This is the starting point
  for continuous pricing adjustments when generating offers.

- **reference_price** is an attribute of a `Demand`. It is the pricing anchor point for customer
  choice processes among customers in that segment for the given market. The `reference_price` is
  typically the lowest `fare_price` for any fare on any carrier serving that market, regardless of
  whether that fare is actually offered to the customer or not. However, this is configurable, and
  in some cases the `reference_price` may be some other value, or some multiple of the lowest
  `fare_price`.

- **decision_price** This is an "average" price attached to a `PathClass` (paths by booking class)
  or `Bucket` (legs by booking class). This is the default value that is fed as the input into all
  RM steps that need to know the price of various booking classes that may be opened or closed. The
  algorithm which determines how this average is calculated and how often it is updated is
  configurable.

- **marginal_rev** This is the expected positive marginal revenue to the carrier from keeping a
  booking class open on a path or leg. For cases where expected marginal revenue is negative, this
  value is zero, as we assume that the booking class will definitely be closed. These values are the
  result of "fare adjustment" algorithms (e.g. "MR" or "KI") when used with variations of
  forecasting that account for the probability of sell up (e.g. Q or conditional). This is a vector
  of values by timeframe, as the probability of sell up is not constant over time. If using
  "standard" forecasting that has independent demands, the marginal_rev can still be expressed as a
  vector but all the values in the vector are equal.
